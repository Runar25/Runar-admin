# 1. URUZ — Mótsognir

## 1.1 What Uruz means (sources, not from my head)
- [K]: **raw power, strength, primal force.**
- [POP]: "Uruz přichází z místa, kde ještě není hotový tvar." (comes from a place where there is no finished shape yet) · force of body, earth and a life that does not stop because the road is uncomfortable · a wild aurochs, heavy, untamed · "Je to síla, kterou poznáš ve chvíli, kdy ji potřebuješ." (a force you recognise at the moment you need it) · a period that forces you to change: the old is no longer enough, the new has no shape yet; Uruz brings "schopnost proměny" (the capacity for transformation) · something raw, not smoothed by experience or reason, sometimes uncomfortable: it makes you meet your own force and its limits · "Neříká: buď silnější."
- **The question [POP] ends on:** here is a strength that is already in you — what can come of it?
- [DES]: Mótsognir is „první a největší z dvergů" (first and greatest of the dwarfs), (Agndofa) „v nejhlubší skále pod středem ostrova" (in the deepest rock under the middle of the island). [VOL] 10 (file 01): "Þar var Mótsognir mæztr um orðinn dverga allra" — "created greatest of all the dwarfs" (Thorpe). That is all the source says about him.
- **Images Rúnar already has for Uruz** [IMG]: *"You lift the end no one else could get under, and it comes up."* · *"The bull turns in the pen and the whole fence moves with him."* · *"You lean into the boulder in the home field, and it shifts."* · *"The bull tears itself up out of the bog and keeps going."* · *"You drive the iron bar into the frozen ground with all your might, and it gives way."* (and a reindeer stag, which the marker list above excludes). Reference only.

## 1.2 Three angles

| | Angle | What Mótsognir does | How it carries the meanings | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Planted (v0) | stands at a cave mouth, one hand on the rock as if it were his own body | strength that need not be shown; rawness (unadorned) | a still figure may read only as "old and big" |
| **B** | Leaning in | shoulder against a boulder in the home field; it has just begun to shift | "strength you recognise when you need it"; the doing | shows effort — [POP] says this is not a force that is *performed* |
| **C** | Unfinished rock | planted before a basalt face where the columns are only half split, with half-formed shapes in the stone | "no finished shape yet"; transformation; origin (he is the first dwarf) | the shapes in the stone may look eerie, or like carved faces |

## 1.3 Recommendation: A with the rock face from C; B kept as the angle to test against

A and C together carry the most of [POP] with one dwarf and one backdrop. B is the only angle that shows the verb of the rune ("the force you recognise when you need it"), so I would still **generate and test it side by side with A+C**.

**The scene A+C (foreground → background):**
- *Foreground:* his **hand flat on the rock**, stone dust on the knuckles. A pile of loose, unshaped basalt at his feet.
- *Middle:* Mótsognir **standing planted**, feet apart, in an aurochs-hide mantle, **no embroidery and no cuffs on the braids** (02a, sheet 02). Eyes on the rock, not on the viewer.
- *Background:* a **basalt wall where the columns are only half split**: some have clean hexagonal edges, some are still rough and merged. Dark cave mouth to one side.
- *Light and colour:* low dim light from the cave side; obsidian and hearthwood (hide-brown) dominate; one thin line of ember-gold warm light on the rock edge. The pendant is a **raw, unpolished stone** with ᚢ in whisper-blue — the only saturated colour.
- *Gesture/gaze:* planted, weight low; **no raised arm, no weapon**.

Elements: hand on rock (strength known by contact) · hide mantle and unadorned cloth (raw) · half-split columns (no finished shape yet; transformation) · the dwarf's size against the wall (primal, first) — **four**, well under the limit. Not in the scene: aurochs animal (the hide mantle stands for it).

## 1.4 What to avoid
- A brute or barbarian look; spikes, a raised weapon, clenched fists.
- **Do not make it a comic "gruff mountain dwarf"** — [POP]'s register is not boastful.
- No aurochs in the frame (it would take over the card; and no wild aurochs lives in Iceland).
- Not a statue: he must read as alive and weight-bearing.
- Do not let the half-formed stone become faces or limbs (the first test image should check this).

## 1.5 Prompts (variable part)

> **Recommended (A+C):** *Mótsognir, the largest and oldest dwarf, enormous and rough-hewn, wearing an aurochs-hide mantle over plain stone-grey and hide-brown cloth with no embroidery, thick iron-grey braids without metal cuffs, stone dust on his beard and knuckles; he stands planted with his feet apart before a wall of black basalt whose columns are only half split — some with clean hexagonal edges, others still rough and merged — one flat hand resting on the rock as if it were his own body; loose unshaped basalt at his feet; a dark cave mouth to one side; dim light with a single thin line of ember-gold on the rock edge; his obsidian pendant is an unpolished raw stone carrying ᚢ in whisper-blue light.*
>
> **B (leaning in):** *Mótsognir, enormous rough-hewn dwarf in an aurochs-hide mantle, shoulder set against a large boulder in a snow-patched home field, the boulder just beginning to shift, a faint furrow of displaced earth under it; his face calm rather than strained; raw unpolished stone pendant with ᚢ.*

## 1.6 Strongest argument against
- **Uruz is the rune where the verb matters** (an aurochs charges; the fence moves). A planted figure risks "passive". If A+C reads as a handsome portrait of a big dwarf, B is the correction — but then it shows strength being *demonstrated*, which [POP] avoids.
- **The source gives me one line** ("greatest of all the dwarfs"); everything else — the rough finish, the half-formed stone — is [naše]. It must not be presented as Norse tradition.
- **Mótsognir = the first = the biggest** can pull toward "ancestor / elder" (Othila #23, Durinn). The unadorned cloth and the half-split basalt are what keep them apart.

## 1.7 What I need from the owner
Pick A+C, B, or generate both. And: is the **unadorned** treatment (no embroidery, no cuffs, raw pendant) acceptable as the one deliberate exception to the family look?

# 2. GEBO — Alfrigg

## 2.1 What Gebo means
- [K]: **gift, companionship, giving and receiving, balance.**
- [POP]: "Gebo je dar, který vzniká mezi dvěma stranami." (a gift that arises between two sides) · one hand gives, the other receives, and in that moment something arises that was not there before: relation, exchange, obligation, connection · a gift has meaning because something leaves your hand — time, trust, attention, work, part of yourself · you may also receive something without yet knowing what to do with it · "Gebo proto není obchod. Není v ní účetnictví" (Gebo is not trade; there is no bookkeeping) · reciprocity: two forces meet and between them a space arises that belongs to both · freedom: a gift that is forced is not a gift.
- **The question [POP] ends on:** what are you willing to give without certainty that it returns — and what can you receive without immediately having to give something back?
- [DES]: **"the object, not the price"** — the necklace is, in [DES]'s words, „zlato spletené ze čtyř pramenů, co drží jen dohromady" (gold woven from four strands that hold only together); „Co za něj dáš, se ukáže potom" (what you give for it shows afterwards) is a transaction and slides into debt (a documented slip, `RUNAR_EVAL_LOG.md` 2026-08-19/20, as cited in [DES]).
- **Source:** [SÖR] (file 01): Alfrigg is named first of four dwarfs forging a gold necklace; Freyja gets it by lying a night with each. **The one story we have is a purchase; the card must show only the object.**
- **Images Rúnar already has for Gebo** [IMG]: *"One hand holds something out, and the other receives it."* · *"On a cold evening two share one blanket, each pulling it a little less their own way so it covers both."* · *"After the storm the neighbours dig out each other's doors, first one house, then the other."* · *"The sea gives and takes on the shore in the same breath."* Reference only.

## 2.2 Three angles

| | Angle | What Alfrigg does | How it carries the meanings | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Offering (v0) | holds a braid out in one open hand; the other hand open; a second hand enters the frame | two sides; gift; open hands | reads as "he gives, the other takes" — one-directional, easily a sale |
| **B** | The moment of passing | **both hands — his and the receiver's — are on the braid at once**, neither fully holding it | the moment between; "a space that belongs to both"; the strands hold each other | whose hand is the second one? may read as a handshake or a theft |
| **C** | Two ends, slack | each of two hands holds **one end** of the braid; the braid between them hangs slack | each pulls a little less so it covers both; balance; no bookkeeping | the braid may look like a rope or a leash |

## 2.3 Recommendation: B

B is the only angle in which **nobody owns the object at the instant of the picture**: "something that cannot be owned" ([POP]). It also keeps the necklace's own logic — **four strands that hold only because they hold each other** — as the object being held by two.

**The scene (foreground → background):**
- *Foreground:* the **braid of four thin gold strands** held between two hands; **his hand and one other hand, both at the middle of the braid**. The strands are visibly four, each slightly different in tone, twisted into one.
- *Middle:* Alfrigg, slender, **looking at the receiver (out of frame to one side), not at the braid and not at the viewer**; open, ready face; **four thin gold strands woven into his own beard** (the echo; 02a sheet 07).
- *Background:* warm hearth-gold light on stone, a hint of a forge or a stream — **nothing that looks like a market, a scale, a counter, a table with goods**.
- *Light and colour:* ember gold dominates; aurora-green accents on the coat; the pendant carries ᚷ in whisper-blue.
- *Gesture/gaze:* both open; **no closed fist anywhere**; the receiver's hand is a **dwarf's hand** (same scale, same cuffed wrist), so the second figure is not a goddess.

Elements: two hands (two sides) · four strands (reciprocity, the object) · the gaze toward the receiver (relation) · absence of any price (not bookkeeping) · open hands (freedom) — **five**.

## 2.4 What to avoid
- **Anything that shows a price:** coins, scales, a ledger, a bed, a night, a bargain pose, a hand-over with a stack of goods in return ([DES]; [v0] never).
- Do not draw **Freyja or any woman** as the second hand: that pulls the card into [SÖR]'s story.
- No ribbon or rope look — the strands must read as gold wire woven.
- No ring or crown.
- No handshake pose.

## 2.5 Prompts (variable part)

> **Recommended (B):** *Alfrigg, slender dwarf with four thin gold strands woven into his beard, looking toward another dwarf who is out of frame to one side; in the foreground a braid of four fine gold strands is held at its middle by two hands at once — his, and the cuffed hand of the other dwarf — neither hand gripping, all fingers open; warm hearth-gold light on stone; ember gold coat with aurora-green trim; obsidian pendant with ᚷ in whisper-blue light.*
>
> **A (v0):** *Alfrigg, slender dwarf holding out a braid of four woven gold strands in one open hand and offering the other hand open, a second hand entering the frame to receive; warm golden light; obsidian pendant with ᚷ.*
>
> **C (two ends, slack):** *Alfrigg and another dwarf's hand each holding one end of a long braid of four gold strands, the braid hanging in a gentle slack curve between them, neither pulling; warm light.*

## 2.6 Strongest argument against
- **B may still read as a transaction.** Two hands on one object is also what a handover looks like; without the price, the viewer supplies one. A is clearer about *giver and receiver*, B is clearer about *between* — the first test image has to show which the viewer sees.
- **Counting to four is hard for image generators.** If the strands come out as two or six, the object loses its meaning; the beard strands may confuse it further. Fall back: "a gold braid" without a count, and keep the number only in the description.
- **The source dwarf's story contradicts the rune.** The only thing we know of Alfrigg is that he sold a necklace for a night. Our card takes the object and discards the story. That is defensible (the owner's rule) but it is the one place where the myth is the inverse of the rune.
- **The rune is about a relation; the card has one named character.** The second hand is unnamed and undefined; it may need to be a recognisable, non-threatening presence (another dwarf).

## 2.7 What I need from the owner
Pick B, A, or both for a first test. And: may the second hand belong to **another dwarf of the set** (it would link the cards), or must it stay anonymous?

# 3. THURISAZ — Þyrnir

## 3.1 What Thurisaz means
- [K]: **gateway, threshold, caution, thorn, protection, disruptive force.**
- [POP]: "Thurisaz stojí u dveří." (stands at the door) · not yet a step in or out: a moment when something stops and that creates space to see what is going on · the force of the thorn: a sharp boundary that neither says "come in" nor "go away", but "attention" · usually tied to impact, conflict, protection and "silou, která dokáže rozbít to, co už nemá držet pohromadě" (a force that can break what should no longer hold together) · the hardness is not necessarily an attack: sometimes it is the obstacle that protects you from a step you would have taken too fast · the moment between impulse and act · "Ještě chvíli. Podívej se, co se právě otevřelo." (A moment more. Look at what just opened.) · behind the sharp edge there need not be an enemy — there may be something you have not yet seen.
- **The question [POP] ends on:** what appears when this time you do not strike back?
- **Rúnar's images** [IMG]: *"The crack in the lava waits — you cannot cross it without a jump."* · *"You take hold of the door handle — and stop."* · *"The ram lowers its head at the gate, and you wait before you open it."* · *"The nettle grows by the door; whoever goes past too fast gets stung."* · *"The thorn hedge lets nothing through without cost."* · *"The quarrel at the table goes quiet, one word short of boiling over."* · *"A thorn tears your sleeve one step short of the gully's edge."* Reference only.

## 3.2 Three angles

| | Angle | What Þyrnir does | How it carries the meanings | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | The pass (v0) | stands in a narrow pass between two black lava ribs, one flat palm raised | threshold; thorn; pause; something unseen beyond | the raised palm is turned **to the viewer** = "stop" aimed at us = a command |
| **B** | The latch | in a thorn-hedge gate, **his hand on the latch, not opening** | between impulse and act (his, not ours) | the "door" motif returns |
| **C** | The cracked rib | stands beside a lava rib split by a crack; a thorn bush at the gap | the force that breaks; the thorn; a pause | the crack dominates; he is only a bystander |

## 3.3 Recommendation: A with two corrections, and one element from C

**Correction 1 — whom the palm is for.** In v0 the flat palm is a gesture to the viewer. A card that tells the viewer "wait" is advice, and the brief's criterion 5 asks for an image that does not push an answer. **Turn the palm toward the opening, not toward us, and put his gaze on what is opening.** Then it is *his* pause, and we are shown a pause rather than ordered into one — the same way [POP] puts it ("ještě chvíli" is said to the person in the reading, not by the picture).
**Correction 2 — the missing meaning.** [POP]'s force that breaks what should no longer hold is the one meaning v0 drops. Add **one lava rib visibly cracked through**, so the wall of the pass has been broken once already. It is a single element and does not make him hostile.

**The scene (foreground → background):**
- *Foreground:* **a blackthorn branch** with a few thorns, close to the lens; his **flat palm turned toward the gap**, fingers up.
- *Middle:* Þyrnir, lean and wiry, **looking at the gap, side-on to us**, scratches on the forearms; blackthorn staff in the other hand, tip on the ground.
- *Background:* the narrow pass between two black lava ribs, **one rib cracked through**; in the gap a **faint warm light**, source not visible.
- *Light and colour:* dusk; obsidian dominates; hearthwood coat with ember-gold stitching; the warm gap is the only strong light; the pendant carries ᚦ in whisper-blue.
- *Gesture/gaze:* firm, not hostile; **no weapon raised, no clenched hand, no frown**.

Elements: pass (threshold) · blackthorn (thorn) · palm (pause) · faint light in the gap (not yet seen) · cracked rib (the force that breaks) — **five**, at the upper limit.

## 3.4 What to avoid
- Menace: no snarl, no bared teeth, no armoured gate-guard, no spear.
- A fight, a tackled intruder, a person running toward the gap.
- **Do not show what is behind the opening** — [POP]: "something you have not yet seen".
- Do not turn the thorn into a spiked-crown or spiked-armour look.
- Do not mix with Eihwaz (#13) — Thurisaz is a **pass in rock**, Eihwaz is no longer a doorway (see 02b).

## 3.5 Prompts (variable part)

> **Recommended (A + cracked rib, palm toward the gap):** *Þyrnir, a lean wiry dwarf with scratched forearms, dark brown hair streaked grey and a short pointed beard, in a hearthwood coat with ember-gold stitching, standing side-on in a narrow pass between two black lava ribs at dusk, looking at a faint warm light in the gap beyond; his one flat hand raised with fingers up, palm turned toward the gap, not toward us; his other hand on a blackthorn staff; a blackthorn branch with thorns close in the foreground; one of the lava ribs is cracked right through; firm, calm, not hostile; obsidian pendant with ᚦ in whisper-blue light.*
>
> **B (the latch):** *Þyrnir, lean dwarf with his hand on the iron latch of a wooden gate set in a thorn hedge, not opening it, head slightly turned as if listening for what is on the other side; dusk, low light; blackthorn staff in his other hand.*
>
> **C (the cracked rib):** *Þyrnir standing beside a black lava rib that is split by a crack, a blackthorn bush growing in the gap, one palm raised flat toward it, dusk light.*

## 3.6 Strongest argument against
- **The correction makes the gesture less legible.** A palm raised toward the viewer is the clearest "wait" there is; a palm turned to the side may read as "he is pointing at something" or "greeting". The first test image should check what people see.
- **Five elements is the ceiling.** The cracked rib is the most expendable; if the figure disappears, drop it first (it is [POP]'s least central meaning for the card).
- **A guardian at a gate is a trope.** The lava pass, the blackthorn and the side-on gaze are what keep it from "generic gate-warden".
- **The meaning "protection" can read as "he defends something"**, which is the opposite of letting people look at what has opened.

## 3.7 What I need from the owner
Keep the **drop of the destructive side** or accept the cracked rib? And: is turning the palm away from the viewer acceptable as a general rule for all cards (the dwarf never addresses the viewer with a gesture)?

# 4. DAGAZ — Þjóðreyrir

## 4.1 What Dagaz means
- [K]: **turning point, dawn, breakthrough, light, transformation.**
- [POP]: "Dagaz je okamžik, kdy se noc láme ve světlo." (the moment the night breaks into light) · the land was dark; then a first pale strip appears on the horizon: nothing happened in one big movement and yet you know a day is coming · the rune of transformation, breakthrough, a new view and passage from one state to another · "Nejsi už tam, kde jsi byl, ale ještě nestojíš úplně na druhé straně." (you are no longer where you were, but not yet entirely on the other side) · it can be quiet: suddenly you understand; suddenly you see the same thing from the opposite side; suddenly the question you asked for a long time is no longer the same question · not a light that solves everything: a change of perspective after which you can no longer see the world quite as before · "Možná už stojíš v jejich prahu." (perhaps you already stand on its threshold)
- **The question [POP] ends on:** what in you has changed enough that the same world in front of you begins to show itself differently?
- **Source:** [HAV160] (file 01, via a secondary article): "In Hávamál 160 it is said that the dwarf Thjódrørir sang before Delling's doors" (C. Tolkien, 1960); he may have been "giving warning to his people that the sun was coming up"; Lindow notes the "doors" may be a sunrise metaphor. **The stanza text itself I did not see.** [DES] calls him "the hinge where the hidden turns into the revealed" and "the only dwarf tied to the day".
- **Rúnar's images** [IMG]: *"You stand high in the pass between two valleys; behind you it is still dark, and ahead it has grown light."* · *"At midnight all goes quiet a moment; then the birds sing again, and a new day has dawned."* · *"Dawn comes without your noticing when night became day."* · *"The first glimmer returns after the blackest midwinter dark."* · *"You wake, and dawn has already changed the light in the room."* · *"The last wall of rock in the tunnel is blasted, and daylight shines in from the other side."* Reference only.

## 4.2 Three angles

| | Angle | What Þjóðreyrir does | How it carries the meanings | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | The doorway (v0) | stands in a stone doorway at a fjord's edge; half the face in blue night, half in the first gold | threshold; "not yet across"; half/half | doorway motif (Othila, and Eihwaz if it keeps its door) |
| **B** | The saddle | stands in a mountain saddle between two valleys: dark behind him, lit ahead | "no longer where you were, not yet across" literally | the dwarf is small against a wide landscape; the face is not lit |
| **C** | The blue hour | on a black beach at the end of night; a gold line on the sea horizon lights half his face; no architecture at all | the same meanings without a door | less distinctive; "man on a beach at dawn" |

## 4.3 Recommendation: A, with the doorway as a natural basalt arch; B kept as a test angle

The **doorway is the source's own hook**: the one line we have about Þjóðreyrir puts him "before Delling's doors". A is also the only angle that shows the rune's gesture in the *face*, where the viewer looks. To avoid the doorway repeating on four cards, **I propose to move Eihwaz away from a door** (02b) and to make this doorway a **natural basalt arch at the edge of a fjord**, not built masonry — so it reads as landscape.

**The scene (foreground → background):**
- *Foreground:* his **hand resting on the stone edge of the arch**, not gripping; a few pale grasses at the base.
- *Middle:* Þjóðreyrir, **standing inside the arch**, face turned a little toward the horizon: **half the face in blue night, half in the first gold**; the other hand open at his side.
- *Background:* a fjord with steep bare mountains; **one thin line of pale gold on the horizon** over still water; a few last stars in the upper part.
- *Light and colour:* rune cloth blue turning into ember gold at the edges of coat and beard; the pendant carries ᛞ in whisper-blue — **kept small so it does not compete with the gold**.
- *Gesture/gaze:* receptive; **no raised arms, no triumphant stance**; he looks, he does not announce.

Elements: the arch (threshold) · the gold line (night breaking into light) · the half-lit face (change of perspective) · hand on the stone (not yet across) · open hand (receptive) — **five**.

## 4.4 What to avoid
- A **dazzling sunrise, sun disk or rays**; the anchor in [DES] — dwarves are not in full sunlight — is respected here (the sun has not risen).
- Raised arms in glory, a messenger pose, a cock crowing.
- **A door that looks like a church or a castle gate.**
- Reading as sunset (the blue half must be night, and the sky above must still have stars).
- Do not make the face half a mask (a flat split down the middle); the light should fall naturally across it.

## 4.5 Prompts (variable part)

> **Recommended (A, natural arch):** *Þjóðreyrir, a still, medium-built dwarf with deep blue-black hair and a simply braided beard, wearing a coat of deep rune-cloth blue that turns to ember gold at the edges, standing inside a natural basalt arch at the edge of a fjord just before sunrise; one hand resting on the stone of the arch, the other open at his side; one thin line of pale gold on the horizon above still water lights the left half of his face while the right half remains in blue night; steep bare mountains across the fjord, a few last stars high up; calm, receptive, no dazzling sun; obsidian pendant with ᛞ in small whisper-blue light.*
>
> **B (the saddle):** *Þjóðreyrir standing in a high mountain saddle between two valleys, the valley behind him still in blue darkness, the valley ahead just beginning to turn gold; he stands in the middle, one hand open at his side, looking ahead; steep bare Icelandic mountains, a thin line of pale light on the far ridge.*
>
> **C (the blue hour):** *Þjóðreyrir standing on a black volcanic beach at the end of night, a thin gold line on the sea horizon lighting half his face, still water, last stars above, no building.*

## 4.6 Strongest argument against
- **The arch is not what the source says.** "Delling's doors" may be a metaphor for sunrise (Lindow) and not a place the dwarf stood. A literal door is our reading; B and C do not claim the doors at all.
- **A half-lit face is easy to ask for and hard to get from a generator.** It may come out as a flat split or a mask. Test it first; if it fails, B or C keep the idea without the face.
- **Dawn is the cliché image of "new beginning".** The risk is "inspirational poster": the card must stay quiet ("not a light that solves everything").
- **The same-looking blue and gold appears on Sowilo and Dagaz** (both are light runes). Suðri is **ember gold and obsidian**, Þjóðreyrir **blue turning gold** — I would test that the two cards are distinguishable at thumbnail size.

## 4.7 What I need from the owner
Keep the **doorway** as the lead angle (source hook, but interpretive), or start from B? And: is "natural arch at a fjord edge" an acceptable meaning of "doors", or should it stay a built stone doorway?
