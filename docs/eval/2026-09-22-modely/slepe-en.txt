ZADÁNÍ, KTERÉ DOSTALY VŠECHNY MODELY (totéž, bit po bitu):
- runa Raidho (důraz: natural rhythm) · obraz: ovčí stezka, která se vine sama od sebe · místo: dolů podél řeky mezi svahy
- oblast: Crossroads & Decisions (rozcestí) · hledání: Confirmation → poslední věta má nabídnout DVĚ možnosti, čím to může být tam, kde se cesta člověka dělí
- 4 krátké věty, 50–58 slov, čte se nahlas · jméno Kuky přijde pozdě, u konce · jedna esenční věta: co runa DĚLÁ skrze obraz
- zákaz: neříkat člověku, co ví nebo kterou cestou půjde (kromě poslední věty jako možnost) · žádné cold reading, rada, předpověď

Doslovné řádky zadání:
  DRAWN RUNE: Raidho — focus on: natural rhythm · World: the living moment, what is active now · Elements: Air
  READING ANGLE (follow this entry point — let it shape the opening and tone): Open with the one thing in the image that stays fixed while the rest gives way.
  IMAGE — the picture in this reading comes from here: a sheep-track winding of its own accord. Where: down along the river between the slopes. Let it become your own seeing in the text.
  THE ESSENCE LINE: after the picture, one short line that says what the rune DOES through this image — its sense in plain words a stranger to runes can grasp. The familiar word may live inside the doing ("exchange between the sea and the shore"). Never a fixed formula. No invented mechanism, no fate. Never tell the seeker what it means for them.
  The reading is for Crossroads & Decisions — let the image you were given land where a way divides. Do not tell them what they know or which way they will take — except in the closing line, and there only as a possibility they may weigh.
  End on one line that holds out two things this may be where the seeker's way divides, each a state that may be so, left for them to weigh.

========
### A
A1) The river keeps its course while the sheep-track gives way to each bend between the slopes. Raidho brings movement into accord with the ground as it changes. At the fork, Kuky, the air carries sound from both directions, though neither path reveals its full length. This may be a rhythm still forming, or one already asking to be followed.

A2) The river stays fixed while the slopes soften and the sheep-track winds of its own accord beside the water. Raidho keeps movement close to the ground, where each turn answers the living moment. At the fork, Kuky, the air carries both ways without naming either. This may be a rhythm still holding, or one already changing.

A3) Only the river holds its course as the sheep-track winds between slopes and thins where the ground divides. Raidho shows movement finding its natural rhythm through the living moment. Air passes over both branches, while neither reveals its full length. Here, Kuky, the divide may hold a pace already forming, or a pause not yet complete.

### B
B1) The sheep-track holds its winding line down by the river, while the slopes give way around it and the water keeps moving. Raidho gives this path a rhythm shaped by the ground, not a straight answer at the divide. Kuky, the track leaves the far bank out of view, where the current turns beyond sight. This may be a divide between keeping pace and changing course.

B2) Along the river between the slopes, one bend in the sheep-track stays fixed while the banks give way around it. The track winds by its own accord, marking a course through ground that does not hold still. Raidho gives movement a natural rhythm, shaped by the way forward and the ground it crosses. For you, Kuky, this may be where your way divides between a pace that feels steady and one that feels urgent.

B3) The sheep-track stays fixed as it winds of its own accord down along the river between the slopes, while the banks give way to the current. Raidho traces a natural rhythm through the ground where a way divides. Kuky, the path’s course is visible, but what lies beyond its bend remains out of view. This may be a divide between keeping pace and pausing to listen.

### C
C1) The river holds its course while the sheep-track wanders the slope beside it, veering wherever the ground gives way. Raidho is that steady pull underneath the wandering, the rhythm that keeps moving whether the path is straight or not. Where the track splits along the bank, both ways still run downstream. This may be a fork worth pausing at, Kuky, or simply the same current finding two mouths.

C2) The river holds its line while the sheep-track wanders down the slope beside it, bending where the ground allows. Raidho is that steady pull of water finding its own pace through the turns. The track splits ahead, one branch climbing the bank, one staying low near the current. What stays fixed is the river; the walking is yours to choose, Kuky, and this may be where your way divides between following the water and cutting your own line above it.

C3) The river holds its line while the sheep-track wanders beside it, dipping and rising as the ground allows. Raidho is that steady moving with the land, not against it. Where the track splits near the slope, the water keeps its course below, unbothered by which fork the walker chooses. It may be, Kuky, that one path follows the rhythm already carrying you, and the other asks you to set your own.

### D
D1) The river holds its course between the slopes, while the sheep-track winds beside it and splits where the bank falls away. Raidho gives movement a rhythm without fixing its direction. Air moves along both branches, and neither keeps the river in sight for long. Kuky, this may be a pause before a turn, or a turn already taking shape.

D2) The river stays in its bed while a sheep-track winds between the slopes. Raidho gives movement a rhythm shaped by the ground beneath your feet. Where the track divides, the air moves freely across both branches, and neither hides what lies beyond the bend. Kuky, you may be weighing a pace that feels settled against one still finding its course.

D3) The river holds its course while the sheep-track bends between the slopes, finding room where the ground allows it. Raidho follows the pace a way takes as it forms. At the fork, air moves across both paths, and neither shows its full length. Kuky, your pause may hold a need for steadier ground, or a readiness to move.

### E
E1) The river holds its line while the sheep-track above it wanders, splits, and finds the water again further down. Raidho is the going that keeps its own pace, whatever the ground does. Neither path was measured out; both were walked into being by whoever went first. Kuky, one way may be the longer walk to the same bank, or it may be a different river altogether.

E2) The river holds its line while the sheep-track above it wanders, crossing, doubling, giving way to the slope. Raidho is the going that keeps its own pace, whatever the ground does. Ahead the path forks and the far side stays out of sight. Kuky, either the pace is yours already, or it is still being set by the slope.

E3) The river holds its line while the sheep-track above it wanders, splits, and finds the water again. Raidho is the going that keeps its direction without keeping to one path. Where the slope steepens, two tracks leave the same ground and neither is marked. You may be choosing the way, Kuky, or choosing the pace you take it at.

### F
F1) Before you, the winding sheep-track stays firm as wind bends the grass beside the river. With Raidho, you see a rhythm shaped by the ground. You reach a fork between the slopes, where neither bend reveals its full length. Kuky, you may be choosing between a pace you can sustain and one you cannot yet judge.

F2) Beside you, the sheep-track holds its worn line while wind bends the grass along the river between the slopes. Through Raidho, you see a pace shaped by the ground. At the fork, you cannot see beyond either bend. Here, Kuky, your choice may be between a steady pace and a pace not yet familiar.

F3) At your feet, a stone stays fixed while the sheep-track bends down between the slopes toward the river. Raidho draws your attention to a pace shaped by the ground. Where your track divides, wind moves through both openings. Kuky, your choice may be between a steady pace on familiar ground and uncertain footing beyond the bend.

### G
G1) Down between the slopes, the river holds its course while the sheep-track bends where the ground allows. Raidho is this, movement keeping time with the land. Where the track splits near the water, both branches still follow the river. Kuky, this may be where you are already walking in step, or where you are still finding your pace.

G2) The river keeps its bed, while the sheep-track above it bends along the slope. Raidho is movement that finds its own pace, following the ground instead of forcing a line. Where the track splits, both paths still run beside the same water. Kuky, this may be a choice between two ways, or one way walked at two speeds.

G3) The river keeps its course, and the sheep-track winds beside it of its own accord. Raidho is that rhythm, a way that follows the ground as it is. Where the track splits, both branches still keep the river close. Kuky, this may be a fork where either way runs true, or one where your feet already know the ground.
