=== 1 ===
Halfway across, the planks are wet with spray and the rope rail is cold under your hand. Below you the river runs grey and loud, carrying silt down from the ice. The water never stops, but the bridge does not move with it. Frost holds in the seams of the wood where the sun has not reached. From here both banks look the same distance away. The air smells of stone and snowmelt. You stand on the one still thing in all that rushing, and the river goes on under you.
=== 2 ===
The cave is quiet, and the forge sits at the back of it under a grey blanket of ash. No one works the bellows now. The anvil is cold to the touch, and a hammer lies across it where it was set down. Under the ash the coals still hold a dull red, slow and patient. Water drips somewhere deeper in the rock. The heat does not rise and does not die, it simply waits under its cover. Your breath shows faintly in the cave air, and the ash keeps what it keeps.
=== 3 ===
The rope runs from one bank to the other, tied off around two boulders. Rime has gathered along its length, a thin white crust that cracks when you touch it. The river below is half frozen, with black water moving in a channel down the middle. The rope sags a little at the centre and holds there. Nothing pulls on it now. Wind comes down the valley and the rope barely stirs, stiff with cold. On the far bank the boulder sits exactly where it sat, and the line between them is clear.
=== 4 ===
Halfway across, the bridge sways a little under your weight. The river below is milky with glacial silt, fast and cold, and it carries the ground of the mountains down to the farms in the valley. On the far bank sheep graze the green flats the river built. You can hear a bell on one of them over the noise of the water. The planks are worn smooth in the middle, where many feet have crossed with loads on their backs. What is carried here crosses one step at a time.
=== 5 ===
The cave holds the forge, and the forge holds its fire under ash. Around it lie the things worked here, a bundle of iron bars, a pair of shears half finished, a sack of charcoal that took a summer to burn. Every piece cost something, wood or ore or a long day at the bellows. The coals under the ash are worth more than they look, a single breath of air would bring them up white. The tools hang in their places on the wall. Everything here was gathered and kept.
=== 6 ===
The rope is thick, twisted from horsehair and wool, and it runs taut between two posts on either bank. Someone made it by hand over many evenings. Bundles have crossed on it, hung from a ring that slides along the line, fleeces and dried fish and sacks of grain. The ring is still there, polished bright where it rubs. The river beneath is too fast to wade, so whatever moves from one side to the other moves along this rope. On the far bank a cart stands empty at the post.
=== 7 ===
Halfway across, the sun comes over the ridge and lands full on the bridge. The spray from the river catches it and hangs in the air as a small, broken rainbow. The planks steam where the light warms them. Below, the glacial water turns from grey to a pale bright green, and you can see stones on the bottom that were hidden a moment ago. Your shadow lies sharp across the rail. Both banks are lit now, the near one and the far one, and the river runs loud between them.
=== 8 ===
Light comes in at the mouth of the cave, a long bar of it reaching across the floor toward the forge. It stops just short of the ash. The fire lies banked under grey, holding its heat low where the sun cannot touch it. Two kinds of warmth sit in this place, the one from outside and the one kept in here. The anvil stands between them, half in light and half in shadow. A spark lifts from the ash on some small draught, glows for a moment, and goes out.
=== 9 ===
The rope stretches from bank to bank, bright where the noon sun strikes it and dark where it dips toward the water. It is dry and warm to the touch, the fibres stiff with light. Below, the river flashes so hard it is difficult to look at. The rope casts a thin shadow across the current, a line drawn straight over moving water. On the far bank the post stands in full sun. The whole length is visible from here, every knot and every fray, nothing hidden along it.
