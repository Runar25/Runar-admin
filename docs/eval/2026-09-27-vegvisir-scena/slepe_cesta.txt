1. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm climbing a steep path up a mountain.
   DRAWN RUNE: Jera
   TEXT: The path rises, and Jera lies along it. A steep climb is not taken in one stride. The slope gives ground slowly, a foot at a time, and the summit stays where it is regardless of how hard the legs push. Mountains keep their own season — snow holds the upper rock until it doesn't, and the ground softens when it softens. What you carry up you carry the whole way. The height gained does not vanish when you stop to breathe; it waits under you while the air thins and the light moves across the stone.

2. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm sailing a boat on the sea.
   DRAWN RUNE: Perth
   TEXT: You are sailing, and the sea keeps what it keeps. Under the hull the water goes down further than your rope, and what moves there moves without asking after you. Perth lies in that dark — the cup shaken, not yet tipped. Wind comes from where it comes. A fish rises, a squall passes to the south, a shape breaks the surface once and is gone before you turn your head. You trim the sail to what arrives. What you give the sea and what it gives back sit on the same scale, and the sea does not show you the weighing.

3. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm walking through a forest at dusk.
   DRAWN RUNE: Raidho
   TEXT: Raidho falls on this arm, and the forest takes it. The light is going, and the path holds its shape a little longer than the trees do — you can see where your feet will land, one step ahead, then another. Your pace sets itself here. Roots and stone decide where the stride shortens, where it opens again. Behind you the way you came is already closing into shadow. Ahead the trunks stand further apart, or seem to, and the ground keeps going under you at the speed it keeps.

   DRAWN RUNE: Isa
   TEXT: Isa lies on the stones, and the water that was moving around them has gone hard and white at the edges. You stand with one foot forward and one foot back, and neither has committed. The stone ahead is glazed, and glaze is a thing you test with weight before you trust it. Gebo holds the centre here — the crossing is not yours alone to time, the river has its own say in when the ice gives. Cold air comes off the water. The far bank is visible, and it is not going anywhere.

5. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm walking through a forest at dusk.
   DRAWN RUNE: Raidho
   TEXT: Dusk in a forest thins the path rather than closing it. Raidho lies along the ground here, not as a map but as pace — the road keeps its own rhythm, and your feet either find it or they do not. The light goes grey between the trunks, and the way ahead shortens to a few steps at a time, which is often all a road gives. Gebo waits uncarved at the centre, the meeting held halfway. Behind you the trees close the gap you walked through; ahead they part only as far as you have come.

6. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm sailing a boat on the sea.
   DRAWN RUNE: Perth
   TEXT: The sea holds its depth without showing it. You read the water by what breaks the surface — a fin, a shift in colour, the dark patch that marks a reef you would not have guessed from the swell. Perth is the cup shaken, the lot not yet fallen. Wind comes from where it comes. Some boats round the headland on a fair reach, some wait three days in a cove for the same crossing, and the difference is not always skill. Under the keel, fathoms you will never see.

7. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm climbing a steep path up a mountain.
   DRAWN RUNE: Jera
   TEXT: The slope rises under you, and Jera lies along this arm like a season that cannot be hurried. A mountain gives back exactly what the weather allows, no more. Some days the ground is firm and the distance closes, other days the cloud comes down and you climb without seeing what you gain. The path holds your weight, and you hold to it — neither one carrying the other. What ripens here ripens on its own schedule. Above you the ridge waits, and the light on it changes with the hour.

8. THE WAY the seeker wrote: I'm crossing a river on stepping stones.
   DRAWN RUNE: Isa
   TEXT: Isa lies on this arm, and the river has gone quiet beneath you. The water between the stones has stiffened to ice, and the current you were reading has stopped speaking. Your foot rests on wet rock and the next stone sits there, visible, unmoving, holding its place in the cold. Ice makes the crossing clearer and slower at once — you can see the whole line of stones now, dark against the white, and you can see how far apart they truly are. The far bank waits. The cold holds everything still.
