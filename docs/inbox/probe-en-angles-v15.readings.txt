Fehu comes now, in the season when the waterfall runs full and white at midday, everything it has gathered pouring over the edge at once. This is the moment the herd is counted, the year's growth stands plain before you. What you have kept is enough to move.
Uruz stands at the moment work turns, when what you have been pushing against begins to give. The sea lies flat and blue out to the far skerries, holding a strength that does not strain. Your hands already know their own weight.
A lone cairn stands on the bare pass, each stone set by a hand that came before yours to mark the way through. Thurisaz is that guarding weight at the roots of the path, a thorn turned outward against what would rush you. What are you protecting that no longer needs the guard?
On the cliffs the puffins crowd wing to wing, each carrying a mouthful of fish back to the burrow, and Ansuz is the one call that carries over the surf below. The words you have been holding are meant to be spoken, not swallowed. The air already waits to carry them.
The mountain road curves ahead, dust rising where the wheel has already turned. This is Raidho — the movement that is happening now, not the road behind or the one still imagined. Where are your feet actually carrying you, while your mind walks elsewhere?
Kenaz is the warmth in your hands right now, the itch in the fingers that want to make something before the light shifts. By the stream the angelica stands tall and green, holding its shape in full daylight. What is already lit in you, waiting only for your hands to move?
Gebo settles in the open hands — that loosening in the palms when they neither grasp nor push away. The lava holds the day's sun and gives it back long after, and the crowberry ripens by taking what the ground offers. What are your hands holding onto that they were meant to pass on?
Wunjo marks this stretch of bright nights, when the light does not leave and neither does the ease you feel now. The meadow grass stands warm past midnight, still holding the day. What is good is here, and it is enough.
Hagalaz sits low in the belly, a cold weight where the ground gives way beneath the highland gravel. You feel it in the jaw, in the shoulders that will not come down. What the hail strikes does not ask permission first.
Nauthiz sits low in you, a tightness behind the ribs that will not loosen no matter how you breathe. Below the moor the roots hold fast against the raven's wind, drinking from ground you never see. What are you refusing to name because naming it costs you?
You stand where the north wind cuts through this bright night, and Isa holds the water still in the ground beneath your feet. Nothing moves that you did not first decide would not move. What are you keeping frozen so you never have to watch it go?
Jera arrives the way the lupine climbs the hillside — not all at once, but a slow purple filling the slope through the long light. What you planted earlier is only now showing its colour. Can you let it come at its own pace?
Eihwaz comes as the yew whose roots hold in dark soil while the low gold sun rests on the sea. What arrives here has already done its dying underground, and now asks only to be let up into the light. What have you kept buried that is ready to grow?
Perth is the seed already stirring under the warm slope, where the wild thyme roots take hold before a single pink flower breaks the light. Something is forming in you that has not yet shown its face. Give it the dark a while longer.
Algiz stands like the sedge grass that lifts its blades open to the wide sky, catching the long light while the hay dries below. What reaches upward in you now asks only that you keep still enough to receive it. You are standing in the open, and the light is falling.
Berkana stands where you stand now, between the thing half-done and the thing not yet begun. In the calm fjord a whale breaks the surface, breathes, and slips under again without hurry. What grows in you keeps its own time.
Ehwaz is the horse and rider moving as one across the open highland, the track passable now for the first time since spring. Neither leads; both go. You are already on the move, and the ground holds.
The basalt columns stand in their even ranks along the shore, each one shaped alike yet cut singular by the sea. Mannaz is that standing together while remaining wholly yourself. Which face of you does the salt air reach that the others never see?
There is a loosening low in your belly, the pull of Laguz where water moves under the ground you stand on. The hot spring steams quietly in the green meadow, fed by a source no one can see. You are standing over moving water.
High on the green slope the ewes graze where the grass grows thickest, and beneath that grass the ground holds what will feed them all summer. Ingwaz is that held ground, the seed already set before it shows. What are you keeping quiet until it is ready to stand on its own?
Othila settles low in your chest, the weight of what was handed down before you had words for it. Sun warms the green turf roof of the old farmhouse, and the grass keeps growing whether anyone climbs up to look. You stand on ground others walked first.
Dagaz is the grey lifting off the bog before the first light finds it, that slow turn no hand sets in motion. The cotton-grass stands white and waiting across the wet ground. You are standing in the hour that has not decided what it will become.
You carry a weight with no name, the kind that sits below the wild thyme where the slope holds cool earth the sun never reaches. Blank is that unlit ground beneath the pink — root and water, not yet risen. You stand at the edge of what has not spoken yet.
